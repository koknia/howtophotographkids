Camera Basics & The Four Magic Numbers for Parents

When learning to drive a car or ride a bicycle, you quickly realize "this makes it go, this makes it stop," and so forth. Operating a digital camera is no different.

You have to learn these essential mechanics; there are no magical shortcuts. From my years of running photography workshops for parents, I've seen many moms and dads buy an expensive camera, get overwhelmed by complicated menus, become frustrated by blurry photos, and give up entirely.

Back in the film camera days, you dealt with just a couple of mechanical dials. Today, the sheer volume of menu screens, custom functions, and sub-menus on modern digital bodies can feel daunting. Have no fear—it’s actually very straightforward once you eliminate the noise.

Your ultimate goal is to let the camera handle the minimal, mundane "thinking" while you retain complete control over the key creative decisions.

The Four Magic Numbers

To take complete control of your family portraits, all you need to learn and manipulate are 'The Four Magic Numbers':

  1. Aperture (e.g., f/8 or f/1.8): Controls depth of field and background blur.
  2. Shutter Speed (e.g., 1/60s or 1/500s): Controls motion freeze or motion blur.
  3. ISO (e.g., ISO 200 or ISO 3200): Controls the sensor's sensitivity to light.
  4. Exposure Compensation (e.g., +1 or -1/3): Fine-tunes overall frame brightness.

*(Along with keeping track of the Focal Length of your camera lens, e.g., 50mm or 85mm).*

Buried inside every digital image file is complete technical metadata called EXIF Info. This data records those four magic numbers alongside the date, camera model, and lens focal length. When reviewing your shots on a computer, checking the EXIF data allows you to analyze why a particular shot worked or why it ended up soft.

Set up your camera's viewfinder and back screen to display only these basic facts. When you strip away the secondary icons, operating a camera becomes far less confusing.
